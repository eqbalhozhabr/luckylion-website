One of the most challenging aspects of game design is translating a playtester's "I don't like it" into concrete, actionable improvements. It's a common pitfall to either dismiss such subjective feedback outright or, conversely, to blindly implement a suggested change without understanding the underlying issue. At Lucky Lion Games, we view playtesting not just as a necessary step, but as an art form, especially when it comes to deciphering the nuances of player experience.

Subjective opinions are invaluable, but they often serve as symptoms, not diagnoses. A player might articulate that a specific mechanic isn't fun, but the true insight lies in uncovering *why* it isn't fun. Is it too complicated, lacking sufficient impact, or poorly integrated with other game elements? Our approach involves a deep dive into the "why." We encourage open-ended questions during debriefs, focusing on specific moments in the game where particular feelings arose. Crucially, observation is key; watching player behavior often reveals more profound truths than their spoken words.

We also meticulously look for patterns. While a single subjective complaint might be an outlier, if multiple playtesters express similar sentiments, even in different words, it highlights a systemic design flaw. This systematic approach allows us to move beyond individual preferences and identify objective areas for improvement. By categorizing feedback — for clarity, balance, engagement, or thematic resonance — we can systematically address areas needing refinement. This iterative process of listening, observing, analyzing, and iterating is what transforms raw opinions into truly objective game enhancements, ensuring our games resonate deeply with players.
